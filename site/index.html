VC4QIVerifiable Credentials for Quality Infrastructure

Accreditation certificates, operational scopes, calibration and reference-material certificates can be issued as W3C Verifiable Credentials. VC4QI asks what a verifier may actually rely on when it receives them.

Credentials represent institutional authority and evidence. They do not create it. The verifier selects the profile and decides which answers it needs.

Try it: BAM-M375a

A certified reference material (CuZn39Pb3, arsenic mass fraction) as a signed credential. Your browser runs the repository's own verifier on the signed test credentials, then previews the authority, scope and decision rules still being implemented. Switch the value or tamper with it and watch the verdict change.

The questions a verifier asks

Authentic?

Signed by the issuer and unchanged since.

Valid?

Inside its validity period and not revoked.

Understood?

Units and identifiers follow the verifier's profile.

Authorized?

The issuer may make this claim: an accreditation chain covers it.

Supported?

Required studies exist and come from their own authority.

Fit for use?

The value meets the verifier's own requirement.

Each answer is yes, no or cannot tell. "Cannot tell" never counts as yes, and "authorized" is a separate question from "fit for use".

Project status

VC4QI is research software from BAM. The repository contains a TypeScript reference library with a Python mirror, a browser demo and example credentials. It is migrating to a standards-first reliance model; the new evaluator is being implemented in stages, and the runtime still uses the earlier model.

The BAM-M375a page runs the repository's signature, schema, key and integrity checks on its experimental RM v1 test credentials. Its authority, scope, support and decision steps are a labelled preview of rules still being implemented. The accreditation body, laboratory, operational scope, methods and keys are fictional fixtures. No production service, accreditation infrastructure or external endorsement is claimed.
