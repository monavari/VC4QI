Demonstrator

A verifier receives a credential and has to decide whether to rely on it. These demos let you watch that decision being made: the same evaluator as in the repository, running in your browser on signed test credentials.

  • Nothing is fetchedThe credentials are signed fixtures shipped with the page; there is no server.
  • Six checksAuthentic, valid, understood, authorized, supported, fit for use. Each is yes, no or cannot tell.
  • The verifier decidesChange whom it trusts and the same credential can get a different answer.
  • All fictionalBodies, laboratories, scopes and keys are made up. Only the M375a values are real.

Reference material, top to bottom

The longest walk-through. It follows one certified arsenic value in the reference material M375a (leaded brass) up the chain of authority and asks: may I rely on it?

  • Start with one credential and its links, then build the chain step by step: the state appoints the national accreditation body, Global ACI recognizes it, the body accredits the producer and the laboratory, and the producer certifies and ships.
  • Meet the verifier's profile and its six checks, with an explainer for every credential, link and check.
  • Play scenarios grouped by the question a verifier asks: whom do I trust, when, is the chain complete, is it genuine, is it fit.

Try: suspend an accreditation, withdraw the study, edit a certified value, or choose whom the verifier trusts, and see which answers change.

GS mark to product passport

The same idea for product safety. A GS mark on a product rests on a certificate, test reports and an accreditation; the story lays them out as an actor board, one column per party: the national accreditation body, the scheme owner, the GS body, the manufacturer and the verifier.

  • Build the chain up to a product passport, then verify it.
  • Manipulate it: revoke the body's authority, withdraw the certificate or edit the serial number.
  • See what the manufacturer could reveal, and keep back, when it presents the passport, and how the verifier's own profile decides.

Examples

Four cases, one verifier

A compact page for trying cases quickly: pick a credential, pick whom the verifier trusts, then tamper with the credential or leave one out of what you hand over. The six answers update at once. Choose a case to jump straight to it.

Certified reference material

May I rely on the certified arsenic value of M375a? Three values (178, 197 and 520 mg/kg; the last two are hypothetical reissues), and a verifier that asks either "authorized?" or "fit for use?" against a limit.

Open: As 178 · As 197 · As 520
Calibration certificate

May I rely on each measurement group of a calibration? Four issuers: an accredited laboratory, a laboratory's capability scope, a national institute and a test report. The verifier trusts the accreditation body, the state's appointment or Global ACI's recognition.

Open: accredited lab · capability scope · national institute · test report
GS mark

May I rely on the GS mark on this product? Five products: two tested by the GS body or an external laboratory, a toy outside the scheme's scope, a mark that cites another company's certificate, and a unit claiming another unit's mark.

Open: in-house tested · external lab · outside scope · other company · wrong unit
Product passport

The passport of a unit lists the GS mark in its compliance information: may I rely on that entry? An experimental passport shaped after the EU Digital Product Passport, checked with the accreditation body and scheme owner, or the accreditation body alone.

Open: its own mark credential · on market early · borrowed credential
